Notification transport and update wording

Phase 3 is blocked on the first group. The design record at docs/designs/2026-09-14-notification-transport.md has the full reasoning; this page is the short version with the tradeoffs attached, so it can be answered rather than re-read.

How do subscribers get told?

The page is static and served from object storage precisely because our own infrastructure is what breaks. That decision is what makes this hard, and it is not being reopened to make this easier.

An Atom feed beside the page

Recommended. Free, static, no vendor, no personal data, about a day of work. Adds nothing to the incident path. Most customers do not use a feed reader, so it answers the letter of "subscribe without an account" and probably not its intent.

A hosted email list

What customers would actually use. Unsubscribe, bounces and deletion become a vendor's problem rather than ours. Costs money per subscriber, and puts a third party with its own status page inside the path we use to report outages.

Customer-hosted webhooks

Registered by pull request to the incidents repo. No state of ours and it is what the integration-minded customers want. Serves five customers, not five thousand, and retries need somewhere to live, which is the server we do not have.

Nothing yet, cut the promise

Drop the subscription criterion from the PRD until someone asks for it. Honest, free, and leaves a written promise unkept, which is its own cost.

How should an update read?

The same outage, written three ways. This sets the house style for everyone on call, so it is worth one minute now.

Plain and specific

"Traced to a connection pool exhausted by a slow downstream. Pool size raised."
Says what happened. Assumes the reader can take it, and tells engineers at other companies something useful.

Impact first, cause second

"Checkout is slow for some customers in Europe. Cause identified, fix applied, watching."
Leads with what the reader experiences. Kinder to a non-technical reader, vaguer to a technical one.

Formal

"We are aware of an issue affecting checkout performance and are actively working to resolve it."
What most status pages say. Safe, and says almost nothing; readers have learned to skip it.
