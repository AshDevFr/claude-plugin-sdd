Which severity palette?

Every severity is also a word on the page, so colour never carries the meaning alone. It still has to pass contrast and stay distinguishable for the common colour vision deficiencies. All four below do; they differ in how loud they are.

Traffic light

Resolved Monitoring Partial outage Major outage

What everyone expects, which means nobody has to learn it. Also the loudest: a partial outage reads nearly as alarming as a major one on a phone in daylight.

Muted ember

Resolved Monitoring Partial outage Major outage

The same ordering, desaturated. Reads calmer during a long incident, which is when the page is refreshed most. Slightly harder to tell partial from major at a glance.

Blue to red

Resolved Monitoring Partial outage Major outage

Avoids the green/red pairing entirely, so it survives deuteranopia without relying on the words at all. Costs the instant recognition of green meaning fine.

Monochrome plus one

Resolved Monitoring Partial outage Major outage

Only the two states you can do something about get colour. The quietest option and the most opinionated: it says resolved and monitoring are not news.

How dense should the timeline be?

A long incident collects a lot of updates. This is about what a reader sees before scrolling, at 360 px.

Every update, always

Nothing hidden. A twelve-hour incident is a long scroll, and the reader can see it all.

Latest three, rest behind a toggle

The three most recent, then "show 9 earlier updates". Keeps the incident above the fold. Needs a toggle, which is the page's first piece of interactivity.

Latest only while open, all when resolved

During an incident the last word is all that matters; afterwards the page is a record. No toggle needed, but the page changes shape as an incident closes.

Incident card shape

Bordered card

What it does now. Each incident is a bounded box, which separates them clearly and costs horizontal room at 360 px.

Separated by a rule

No box, a hairline between incidents. Gains back the padding on a phone; the incidents feel like one list rather than several objects.
